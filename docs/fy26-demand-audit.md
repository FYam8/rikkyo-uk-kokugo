# FY26 A/B question-demand audit

Source basis: visible FY26 A/B problem-booklet prompts. This audit does not infer official answers.

## FY26-A literary section

The visible prompt set mixes knowledge/grammar with literary comprehension. It includes blank vocabulary, one-kanji completion, three vocabulary-meaning judgements, subject/predicate and modifier grammar, a 30-character explanation plus feeling choice, phrase interpretation, number-in-kanji, idiom extraction, same-kanji selection, another kanji completion, literary interpretation, and a 60-character synthesis of the president/protagonist/Sayo relationship to books.

The important UX consequence is that a “literary section” cannot be treated as one skill. A single section contains vocabulary, grammar, extraction, choice processing, character psychology and constrained writing.

## FY26-A expository section

Observed tasks include relation/blank selection, contextual vocabulary, numeric-information selection, five-character paraphrase extraction, three-part note completion, dictionary-meaning discrimination, a written explanation of the contrast around dispersal, and two-answer content selection.

## FY26-B literary section

Observed tasks include blank vocabulary, shared-kanji completion, three vocabulary meanings, two-kanji metaphor extraction, <=20-character reason extraction, two-kanji relation completion, character-feeling choice, phrase completion, modifier extraction, and a 60–90-character explanation of the character's reasoning.

## FY26-B expository section

Observed tasks include sentence/blank selection, three-part kanji completion, 25-character extraction of the bias concept, selection of an inappropriate example, vocabulary meaning, explanation of why an 1890s voice may not emerge, two-kanji synonym extraction, sentence insertion, four true/false judgements, and a 100–120-character transfer/application response about school life.

## A/B comparison visible in FY26

Both schedules test the same broad core: kanji/vocabulary + literary reading + expository reading. Both also require extraction and constrained writing. B visibly includes a longer final transfer/application response, while A's final expository task is a two-answer content selection; this is a format observation, not a claim that B is globally harder.

## Shared-engine implication

The common engine should diagnose at the question-demand level rather than by section name alone. Rikkyo data supplies demand tags; the Today/repair/STEP mechanics remain shared upstream.
